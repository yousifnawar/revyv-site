Revyv

Privacy Policy

Last updated 17 November 2026

Revyv is an iPhone app that turns data from your wearable (a WHOOP strap, an Apple Watch or a Fitbit) into sleep, recovery and strain scores. It has no accounts and no servers of its own: your data is kept on your iPhone.

What Revyv reads

  • Your wearable. Heart rate, heart-rate variability, sleep, movement and similar readings, straight from a WHOOP strap over Bluetooth, or from Apple Health for an Apple Watch or a Fitbit.
  • Apple Health, if you allow it: heart rate, HRV, resting heart rate, breathing rate, blood oxygen, wrist temperature, sleep, steps, active energy and workouts. Revyv only reads; it never writes to Apple Health.
  • Google (Fitbit), if you connect your Google account. See the next section.
  • What you enter: your profile (name, age, height, weight), workouts and food you log.

Google user data

If you wear a Fitbit, you can connect the Google account your Fitbit uses. Revyv then asks Google for read-only access to three kinds of Google Health data:

  • activity and fitness (heart rate, HRV, steps),
  • health metrics and measurements (resting heart rate, blood oxygen, breathing rate, skin temperature),
  • sleep (sleep sessions and stages).

Revyv uses this data only to show you your own scores, charts and trends inside the app. It is downloaded straight from Google to your iPhone and stored there. It is not sent to any server run by Revyv, not sold, not used for advertising, and not shared with anyone, except the daily numbers you choose to share with friends (below). People do not read it.

Revyv's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

The sign-in is kept in your iPhone's keychain. You can disconnect in Revyv (Settings → Your device) or remove Revyv's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Friends

The Friends tab is optional and uses Apple's iCloud. If you use it, your username and profile are stored in iCloud so friends can find you. The daily numbers you choose to share (for example recovery, strain, sleep, activities) and your messages are encrypted on your iPhone so only the friend they are for can read them. You choose what to share and can turn it off at any time.

Food search

When you search for a food or scan a barcode, the search words or barcode are sent to Open Food Facts to look it up. Nothing else about you is sent.

Deleting your data

Deleting Revyv from your iPhone deletes everything it stored there. Disconnecting Google in Revyv removes the sign-in. Friends data in iCloud can be removed by deleting your Friends profile in the app.

Children

Revyv is not meant for children under 13.
